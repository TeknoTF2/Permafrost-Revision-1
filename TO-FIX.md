## 1. The Maid Scene — Book One, Chapter 11 (priority line edit)

**Find:** `An absolutely worthless defense`

**Current text:**

> 'An absolutely worthless defense,' Carina thought. She blocked out the maids face in her mind, seeing her as little more than a plank of wood to punch through, as easily as she had done so all her life. And much like the plank of wood Carina imagined in her head, the maid's skull exploded upon impact, through the shield and directly into Andromalius. Her fist connected with Andromalius's jaw while his hammer was mid-swing toward Uthesia, the blow not meant to kill him.
>
> Instead, it was a statement.

**What's wrong:**
1. Typo: "the maids face" → "the maid's face."
2. "through the shield" — the maid *is* the shield, so the referent reads as a third object.
3. Events arrive out of order: the skull "exploded upon impact" before the sentence that throws the punch ("Her fist connected…").
4. "the blow not meant to kill him" dangles — at first read it can attach to the blow that killed the *maid*, which is the worst possible ambiguity at the book's most important moment.

**Suggested replacement (primary):**

> 'An absolutely worthless defense,' Carina thought.
>
> She blanked the maid's face from her mind, the way she'd been trained — a plank of wood, nothing more. She had punched through ten thousand of them.
>
> She punched through this one.
>
> The maid's head burst, and the same fist, barely slowed, caught Andromalius across the jaw while his hammer was still mid-swing toward Uthesia. The punch wasn't thrown to kill him.
>
> It was a statement.

**Alternative (closer to the original single-paragraph shape):**

> 'An absolutely worthless defense,' Carina thought. She blanked the maid's face from her mind — a plank of wood to punch through, the way she had punched through thousands — and drove her fist into it. The maid's head burst. The same punch carried through and cracked Andromalius across the jaw while his hammer was still mid-swing toward Uthesia. It wasn't thrown to kill him.
>
> Instead, it was a statement.

Both versions fix the sequence (blank the face → punch → through the maid → into the jaw → intent),
keep the cold POV-blanking that makes the moment land, and make "not meant to kill" unambiguously
about Andromalius. Note for later: "I'm sorry about the maid" (B1 ch. 21) and the Valdara loot sting
(B2 ch. 5) both lean on this paragraph being crystal clear.

## 2. Tic A — "the kind of X that Y" (42 uses across both books)

**Rule of thumb:** keep it where the *categorization itself* is the insight or the joke
(the knock, the grunt, Ghislaine's grin). Cut it where it's decoration in front of a description
that works on its own. Target ≤1 per chapter.

### Decide intentionality (1)

- **B1 ch. 29** `the kind of laugh that filled a room` (Billy) vs. **B1 ch. 30** `the kind of laugh that pulled you in` (Barbatos/Barbados) — the book's two charm-predators get the same laugh formula one chapter apart. If that's a deliberate rhyme (it's a good one), keep both and consider echoing it once more when they'd be compared. If not, vary one.

## 3. Tic B — "the math / doing the math" (11 uses)

**Recommendation:** make this Gormund's verbal property (it already mostly is — his counting is
characterization), keep Muse's two *thematic* uses, and rephrase the rest so the signature stays his.

### Change these (3)

| # | Location | Find | Suggested fix |
|---|----------|------|---------------|
| 1 | B1 ch. 1 | `She did the math in her head out of habit` | Two "math" uses four lines apart in the opening scene; the second ("already done his grieving over the math") is the stronger image — keep it, and change this one to: "She ran the numbers out of habit." |
| 2 | B1 ch. 14 | `He could see her doing the math` | Ch. 13 just used "did the math in his head" (keep that — it's Gormund). Vary this: "He could see her weighing it — his strength, his blood loss, his odds." |
| 3 | B2 ch. 37 | `at a rate that made the math uncomfortable` | Muse's POV; give Gormund exclusivity: "…burning through his reserves faster than she wanted to think about." |

### Keep these

B1 ch. 1 (Aldrich "done his grieving over the math"), ch. 4 ("the math didn't give a damn about fairness" — Gormund), ch. 8 (Muse hating "the math that said one coughing man was not worth the magic" — this is the theme stated; protect it), ch. 13 (Gormund's bleed-out countdown), ch. 23 ("the expression she'd come to recognize as Gormund doing the math" — the tic *named in-world*, which licenses it). B2 ch. 6, ch. 15, ch. 16 (all Gormund).

## 5. Tic D — Appositive definitions: "the [noun] of a [person] who…" (40 precise uses)

E.g., "The sigh of a woman whose prediction had been wrong." Fine as seasoning at one or two per
chapter, spread out. Two chapters cluster them; fix the clusters, leave the rest.

### Cluster 1 — Book One, Chapter 27 (4 instances; keep 1, rewrite 3)

| Action | Find | Suggested fix |
|--------|------|---------------|
| **Keep** | `precision of a man whose hands needed work` | Strongest of the four — it's Gormund's whole psychology in a clause. |
| Change | `the voice of someone whose mouth had forgotten` | "…fragments, mumbled syllables that would not connect — her mouth no longer cooperating with her brain." |
| Change | `the stillness of someone whose instincts had overridden` | "…her face still, her body locked — instinct overriding muscle." |
| Change | `the voice of a person who was working very hard` | "…her voice flat and controlled, working very hard to keep the fear out of it." |

### Cluster 2 — Book Two, Chapter 19 (3 instances; keep 1, rewrite 2)

| Action | Find | Suggested fix |
|--------|------|---------------|
| **Keep** | `The crying of someone who` | "The crying of someone who doesn't want to be seen doing it" — the chapter's best line; the two rewrites below exist to protect it. |
| Change | `The silence of a person whose expectations` | "She had braced for anger. The question fit nowhere she had prepared, and the machinery of response stalled." |
| Change | `The sigh of a woman whose prediction` | "Uthesia sighed. Her prediction had been wrong, in the best possible way." |

### Going forward

The remaining ~33 instances sit at ≤2 per chapter and read fine individually. The habit to watch
in new drafting: when the construction lands twice within one scene, convert the weaker one to
direct statement (subject + verb), as in the rewrites above — the fix is almost always just
deleting the frame and keeping the payload.
