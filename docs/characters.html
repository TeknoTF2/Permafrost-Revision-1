Reading the Ensemble

The party is built on a classic five-hand structure — leader/wall (Cain), heart/healer (Muse), striker/id (Carina), craftsman/realist (Gormund), specialist/secret (Uthesia) — but what makes it work is that every pairing inside the ensemble has its own distinct chemistry and at least one defining scene: Cain–Muse (the stairwell schism), Cain–Carina (the dungeon argument, "I'm proud of you"), Gormund–Carina (the insult-sibling track), Muse–Uthesia (the held hand, the apology), Gormund–Muse (the stitching scenes, "I believe in you"), Uthesia–Carina (Murim's two exiles, opposite directions), Cain–Gormund (the two veterans, talking craft instead of feelings), Muse–Carina (the older sister Carina names out loud in B2 ch. 29). Ten relationships, all load-bearing. That density is why the Book Two dissolution hurts — and why it works.

The character writing's one systemic weakness: outside the principals, voices flatten (guards, councils, villagers run on a single functional register), and several women in villain orbit (Talia, Jane) are sketched where the men are painted. Both are normal for a draft; both are worth a pass.
