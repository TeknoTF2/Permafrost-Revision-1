The Orbit · The Groomer · The Counter-Mentor

Billy Kidd

A satyr gunslinger in red leather who saves Carina's life, tells her everything she's starving to hear, and charges interest on every word of it.

Role: Antagonist of Carina's soul; the party's dark reflection Origin: Kordovan outlaw; "most wanted bandit crew" pre-dome Signature: Twin golden revolvers, the grin, "darlin'," the philosophy of the full plate Grade: B+

Who He Is

Billy is the manuscript's most morally important supporting character because he is the answer to a question the party never asks: what does Carina's doctrine look like with the charisma turned all the way up and the conscience turned all the way off? His introduction is engineered for reader complicity — he saves a dying girl, the banter sparkles, the "die so full the grave can't hold me" speech is genuinely seductive — and the text then spends Book Two methodically invoicing every charming beat. He looted the storehouses Carina lost her hand opening. He would have "put her out of her misery and robbed the corpse" if she'd been plainer, and says so as a virtue. The wagon scene (B2 ch. 13) is the mask's one full slip rendered with horror-movie economy: spared family, lesson about limits ("It's us or them out here"), forgiveness purchased with flirtation — and then the next traveler shot through the heart mid-sentence after a single screening question. "You got any kids?" — "Um. No, sir." — gunshot. The chapter never editorializes. It doesn't need to.

As a study in grooming mechanics he is uncomfortably precise: isolate (the validation no one else gives), elevate ("command tent privileges," "my left hand," "number one"), reframe (the maid as heroism: "that math adds up just fine"), price the affection (limits are inconvenient; make them up to me), and wait. The final scene — Carina returning with open eyes, asking to be ruined, and Billy's "I'm the best-tasting poison on the continent" — confirms the book knows exactly what it built: he is not a romance, he is a relapse.

Function & Craft

  • Voice: the most differentiated dialect in the book (frontier drawl, endearments as handles, threat delivered at the same temperature as charm). His stories — the bank, the museum, divine wrath that "doesn't show up when you need it" — are funny and they're case studies of his worldview: everything sacred is just unguarded.
  • Thematic position: he completes the mentor spectrum around Carina — Cain (law), Yoo (love failed), Ghislaine (strength as stewardship), Billy (strength as appetite). That she ends Book Two choosing him over all three is the tragedy's proof of damage.
  • Genre work: the Billy-the-Kid/Calamity Jane outlaw iconography drops a spaghetti-western register into the frozen fantasy and it works — the dome's lawlessness is his natural habitat, and "Johnny law's collapsed under a magic apocalypse" tells you the setting's stakes from the predator's side.

Key Scenes

  • B1 ch. 27/29: the rescue, the amputation, the recruitment pitch. Note Jane's "Hasn't even been two days… Can't go a single job without showin' off a new girl" — the text flags the pattern immediately for readers who are listening.
  • B2 ch. 13: the two wagons. The most chilling chapter in the series that contains no monster.
  • B2 ch. 15: the heist pitch — letting Carina volunteer for the suicide run and pricing it as a promotion.
  • B2 ch. 40: "Make me numb. Ruin me." / the kiss. The book's bleakest ending beat, played without glamour.

Critique

  • He needs one dimension that isn't appetite. The character works as a predator-function, but B3 will need either a glimpse of the person under the philosophy (a fear, a loyalty that costs him something — his strange tolerance of Jane's mutinies is the existing seed) or a swift, consequential end. Static charm will diminish on repeat exposure.
  • His crew is a prop. Forty bandits with one named member (Jane) and one corpse (Peter). A second textured crew member would make his world feel less staged for Carina's benefit.
  • Power level is undefined. The revolvers, the draw, the reputation — but he's never been tested on-page against anyone real. The Ghislaine-era Solarspire retreat implies he knows his ceiling; the reader should eventually see it.
  • Tonal guardrail: the Carina relationship must stay framed as predation, never payoff. The current text is on the right side of the line — hold it there, and consider a content note for the book's package.

Verdict — B+. A superbly deployed device-character: the most charming voice in the book wrapped around its coldest function. One more dimension (or one decisive exit) turns him into an A. His final scene with Carina is the cliffhanger that will actually bring readers back — more than the dome, more than the Lords.
